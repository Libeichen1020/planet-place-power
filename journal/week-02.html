Normal or Odd? New York City’s Climate, 1981–2010

The question

How does seasonal temperature and rainfall patterns in New York City compare with the rest of the United States?

The data

I used World Historical Climate - Monthly Averages for GHCN-D Stations for 1981 - 2010 layer, which is based on NOAA’s Global Historical Climatology Network–Daily (GHCN-Daily) Version 3.24 dataset (Menne et al., 2012). The layer contains global weather-station locations and monthly climate data from 1981 to 2010, including average daily temperature, daily maximum and minimum temperature, and precipitation. Stations were included only when they had at least fifteen average daily temperature observations for every month and for at least ten years within the 1981–2010 period. I accessed this public Esri Living Atlas layer on 16 September 2026. This layer’s description reports 4,620 stations, while the current table shows 7,480 features; this mismatch may indicate that the dataset or its documentation has been updated.

The method

I opened the World Historical Climate layer in ArcGIS Online and filtered it to the United States. I used the field GHCN-D ID, the operator starts with, and the value US. I then added a second filter using the field GHCN-D ID, the operator is, and the value USW00094728. I set the condition group to “All of the following are true,” joining the two expressions with AND. This left one weather station located at Central Park, which is used as the proxy for the New York City area.

The evidence

What I found

Global: With the filter removed, the warmest regions appear to be the Sahara, the Middle East, and parts of South Asia. These areas are largely located in the subtropics, where the dry belts associated with descending air pass through. In contrast, the world’s wet belt largely overlaps with the tropical zone. Becasue of Hadley cell, the warm air is heated at equator and ascends into troposphere forms the equatorial low-pressure zone. As the warm air rises, it cools and generates heavy rainfall which therefore creates wet belt along equator. Subsequently, the dry air spread out across towards north and south poles and it descends at around 30° north and south latitude and creats subtropical high-pressure zone and generates dry belt.

Regional: In the United States, the eastern region broadly follows the global pattern of temperature distribution, with warmer climate in the south and colder climate in the north. In the west, however, elevation also plays an important role: the Rocky Mountains form a cold region across Montanna, Wyoming, Colorado and North Arizona. Besides, Alaska is the other cold region because of the high latitude. In terms of precipitation, the wettest areas appear to be Washington State, Oregon State and the Deep South, while the driest areas include southern California, southern Nevada, and southern Arizona.

Site: At New York City, the hottest and wettest month appears to be July. And the coldest month appears to be January while the dryest month appears to be Feburary. The rainfall distributes evenly across the year while the hottest season wetnesses slightly higher rainfall than the cold season. Solely base on these two charts, I assume that New York City has a temperate humid climate. However, the NOAA and the US National Climate Assessment reclassfied the climate of New York City as humid subtropical climate because average summer temperatures of New York City is above 72°F and average winter temperatures staying above 27°F.

Comparison: Although New York City is located in the northeastern United States, its seasonal climate does not fully follow the broader regional pattern. Compared with inland locations at similar latitudes such as Pittsburgh or Chicago, New York City has relatively moderate seasonal temperature variation and precipitation distributed across the year rather than concentrated in one distinct wet season. This makes the city a coastal exception within the regional distribution of climate conditions.

Limits: This map cannot show whether New York City’s climate change is caused by human activities or the natural causes. It only presents monthly averages from one weather station in New York City between 1981 and 2010

What this does not show

The weather station USW00094728 I chose represents only one observation location rather than the entire city. Temperature and precipitation may vary across New York City because of differences in land cover,tree canopy and build environment. Also, the monthly weather data hide the day-to-day variation. One extreme weather event could influence people substantially. Furthermore, we cannot make causal conclusion of the data or what causes the climate change in New York City solely based on the data provided.
