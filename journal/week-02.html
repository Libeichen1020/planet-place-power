Population Density and the Physical Landscape of New York City

The question

How does seasonal temperature and rainfall patterns in New York City compare with the rest of the United States?

The data

I used World Historical Climate - Monthly Averages for GHCN-D Stations for 1981 - 2010 layer, which is based on NOAA’s Global Historical Climatology Network–Daily (GHCN-Daily) Version 3.24 dataset (Menne et al., 2012). The layer contains global weather-station locations and monthly climate data from 1981 to 2010, including average daily temperature, daily maximum and minimum temperature, and precipitation. Stations were included only when they had at least fifteen average daily temperature observations for every month and for at least ten years within the 1981–2010 period.

The method

I opened the World Historical Climate layer in ArcGIS Online and filtered it to the United States. I used the field GHCN-D ID, the operator starts with, and the value US. I then added a second filter using the field GHCN-D ID, the operator is, and the value USW00094728. I set the condition group to “All of the following are true,” joining the two expressions with AND. This left one weather station representing the New York City area.

What I found

What this does not show

This image does not show the daily population change within one area so I had to assume that the daytime population and the night time population are the same. The extent I chose was approximately 1:500000. If I zoomed out one step, the distribution pattern of population density around Manhattan would become clearer but the internal differenciation within Manhattan might not have been observed.
