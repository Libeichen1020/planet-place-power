Pattern, Scale and Density, New York City’s Greenness

The question

What is the green at Washington Square Park, New York City, actually made of, and how do its pattern, scale, and density appear in the Landsat vegetation-index map?

The data

I used Multispectral Landsat Imagery Layer in Arcgis Map Viewer, which includes Landsat GLS, Landsat 8, and Landsat 9 imagery for use in visualization and analysis. The Landsat 8 and 9 imagery in this layer is updated daily and is directly sourced from the USGS Landsat collection on AWS. I also used the Landsat 8 and Landsat 9 images shown in Landsat Explorer, acquired on May 12 2026 and July 16 2026, with a scene cloud cover of 1% and 48% respectively. The sensor’s pixel size is 30 m, so each pixel represents about 900 m² on the ground. I examined the Normalized Difference Vegetation Index (NDVI), calculated from red band 4 and near-infrared band 5: (band 5 − band 4) / (band 5 + band 4). The imagery service is publicly viewable without signing in.

The method

In ArcGIS Map Viewer, I added the Multispectral Landsat Imagery Layer with the NDVI Colorized processing template and kept my site points visible. I then used Landsat Explorer’s NDVI Raw view to read the index because the colorized layer’s popup shows a color-table value rather than the actual NDVI. In Landsat Explorer, I used spectral profile tool in analysis tool to read NDVI values of example site points and my own stie points. I also used the mask tool with a lower threshold of 0.5, so pixels at or above 0.5 were highlighted. This threshold shows relatively high greenness; it does not classify habitat quality. I kept the map extent consistent and included a readable legend and scale bar in the screenshot. I put both map and the screenshot if the map cannot be loaded successfully.

The evidence

What I found

Expectation: In Part 0, I predicted that the picnic point at Governors Island would have the highest NDVI, followed by a fairway at Liberty National Golf Course and the Pond at Central Park . I expect the NDVI valus at Governors Island to be 0.5, Liberty National Golf Course to be 0.2 and the Pond at Central Park to be -0.1. Since the picnic point at Governors Island is mainly covered by trees while it scatters near-infrared back out but the Pond is water surface and it reflects relatively less near-infrared. The actual values were 0.632 at Governors Island, 0.646 at National Liberty Golf Course and 0.218 at Central Park on May 12, when the cloud cover is 1%. Therefore, a fairway at Liberty National Golf Course exhibits the highest NDVI value. I am more worry about the ordering since the size of the gap is smaller than I expected.

Site: At Washington Sqaure Park, the NDVI was 0.798 on May 12 2026, when the cloud cover is 1%. The green of my site is actually made of trees.

City: The NDVI at Washington Square Park is above the botanical garden’s median of 0.517 and above the golf course’s median of 0.600 in the comparison table and Shanghai had a cloud cover of 1.1% on Auguest 22 2025 while New York City had a cloud cover of 1% on MAY 12 2026. I can conclude that my site has a higher measured greenness. This means the pixel at my site reflected more near-infrared light relative to red light on the image date. It does not show that my site has better habitat or more biodiversity, because the comparison uses different locations and use dates; NDVI measures greenness, not ecological quality.

The Named Place. After I set the NDVI value range as 0.5 to 1, on May 12, 2026, the majority of Liberty National Golf Course lit up, which is covered by artificially constructed turf and it is not a natural habitat. Just a couple hundred meters away, the middle section of Liberty State Park does not light up. Actually, it is habitat for around 200 wild white-tailed deers but the trees in that areas were removed and those deers were culled because of the park redevelopment program. The NDVI values at Liberty National Golf Course is approximately 0.6 and 0.3 for Liberty National State Park.

What this does not show

I am using the NDVI at Washington Square Park to stand for the amount of living habitat at my site, and it might fail because the index can give the park’s artificially maintained lawns and its tree-covered areas similarly high values even though they differ in vegetation structure and ecological function. Secondly, the sensor records the light reflected from the upper surface of vegetation; it does not identify plant species or show the what is under the canopy. Therefore, the map is not evidence that the parts of the park with the highest NDVI provide the highest-quality habitat. Besides, it only represents NDVI on one acquisition date, so the observed pattern may change with vegetation season, weather etc.
